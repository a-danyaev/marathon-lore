CURATOR>File 12. References and easter eggs.

12.1

ARG

Puzzles in the real worldTerminal hunt

Bungie has run two ARGs. Before launch, fans dug through "leaked" documents from the "Goliath" project. The main ARG ran in spring 2026, and its prize was the Cryo Archive. canon

How the hunt went

18 hidden terminals, six on each starting map and dressed in authentic '94 interfaces, had to be found and activated in the right order. Community-wide stages came next: poetry, maths, image processing. The sixth took eight mini-puzzles and a 19-page technical manual, and the finale was a shared job: the whole community had to bring down 500 million UESC units. canon

The push was led by Breachers of Tomorrow (breacher.net, 1,500+ members), and the press compared it to "I Love Bees" from the Halo 2 days. On March 20 the Cryo Archive opened to everyone. The one recruiting through those terminals all along was Durandal, now voiced by Ben Starr. canon

Cryo Archive after the ARG

Over the summer the zone became the test bed for the game's first experimental PvE mode. Vault Breaker (Update 1.1.5, July 21) is a chain of vaults for solos, duos and trios; its window was extended to August 18, and after Nightfall Refresh the mode returns October 20 - November 9. canon

The Cryo Archive has seven vaults, and Subject01 waits in the seventh. You can check that in the game itself. canon

The ARG is still running

The official site has hidden pages. Its 404 page answers with a riddle about Prometheus the thief, and the riddle retells a 1994 terminal. The planet's surface data sits on a separate site, tauceti.world, dressed up as a UESC recon portal. canon

Strauss is back

A villain from 1994 has come back too. Dr. Bernhard Strauss, the architect of rampancy who once drove Durandal mad on purpose, is in the lore again, and he brought his book "On AI" (first published in 2472). Strauss holds that large language models shuffle the data they have absorbed without understanding it. canon

For the pedants: UESC Marathon did carry eleven AIs, but the databases do not confirm a separate Strauss memo tying that number to a rampancy instruction. Sources: marathonwiki.com, tauceti.gg.

12.2

Debt

Nightfall Refresh // 06.10.2026The wipe as an invoice

Update 1.1.9.2 "Nightfall Refresh" reset every Runner's progress, and Bungie staged the reset inside the fiction. ONI announces that the fiscal season is over: credits and gear went toward Runner debt, and Cradle and faction upgrades were rolled back. canon

ONI // COLLECTION06 OCT 2026 // PARAPHRASE

> FISCAL SEASON: CLOSED

> CREDITS, GEAR: APPLIED TO RUNNER DEBT

> CRADLE AND FACTION UPGRADES: REVOKED

> LUX, SILK, COSMETICS: EXEMPT FROM COLLECTION

Written off

  • Runner, Cradle and faction levels
  • Priority Contracts and Rank
  • Vault contents
  • Zone access

Kept

  • LUX and SILK
  • Cosmetics
  • A free NuCaloric Reward Pass on top

Why it counts as a reference

Runner debt was in the lore before, but we tagged the fate of lost loot as a fan reading, since the Codex never spelled it out (tip from u/N1349m, file 10). Now there is an official in-fiction framing: the wipe is written up as debt collection. canon

Bungie hasn't said what this means for future wipes. Our guess is that later resets will get the same bookkeeping treatment. theory

12.3

Halo

The Bungie family treeMarathon in Halo

After Marathon, Bungie made Halo and carried the old universe's symbol, names, weapons and even maps across.

  • The Marathon symbol

    The series emblem is hidden all over Halo: on Captain Keyes's badge and pipe, in the "eye" of 343 Guilty Spark, between the letters of the Halo logo. It also turns up in plain sight as the Forerunner "Reclaimer" glyph. canon

  • MJOLNIR armor

    Marathon's cyborgs were called Mjolnir Mark IV. In Halo the name belongs to the Spartan armor Master Chief wears. canon

  • Cortana's rampancy

    Cortana's arc in Halo grew out of Marathon's rampancy. Durandal and Cortana are both named after swords of legend. During Halo's own ARG, Cortana wrote letters to the Marathon fan community. canon

  • Weapons, brands and 117

    The MA-75B rifle became Master Chief's MA5B. The SPNKR rocket launcher (classic players nicknamed it the "Lazyboy" for its armchair-shaped HUD sprite) moved over to Halo; the 2026 game has no rocket launchers at all, and the SPNKR lives on only in the lore. The "Misriah Armory" brand is named after the Misriah massacre. canon

    The Marathon Infinity level "Hats Off to Eight Nineteen" is an official nod to fan archivist Hamish Sinclair (H is the 8th letter, S the 19th). canon Later the community noticed that 819 divided by Bungie's sacred seven gives 117, as in John-117. That part is fan numerology. theory

  • Vidmaster: a challenge from 1995

    Jason Jones coined "Vidmaster" in January 1995 on the Usenet groups comp.sys.mac.games and alt.games.marathon. The rule: finish the game on Total Carnage with one pistol and x1 health. Thirteen years later it became achievements in Halo 3 and ODST, and all seven Vidmaster challenges together unlocked the Recon armor. Source: halopedia.org. canon

  • Halo maps built from Marathon levels

    Halo's multiplayer map "Foundation" copies the geometry of the Marathon level "Thunderdome", and "Gemini" is rebuilt from "Duality". The whole layout came across, recognisable corridors included. Source: halopedia.org. canon

  • The "Marathon Man" achievement

    Halo 3 awards the "Marathon Man" achievement for finding and opening every hidden terminal in the game. It points back to the terminals Bungie has told its stories through since the first game. Source: halopedia.org. canon

12.4

Oni and Destiny

Beyond HaloQuotes across franchises

Bungie kept dragging pieces of its universe into Oni and Destiny, sometimes as a word-for-word quote, sometimes as a cut weapon that lived on in another game.

  • The Marathon symbol in the Oni trailer

    In Oni (Bungie, 2001) the Marathon glyph hangs on warehouse crates in the first trailer and shows up again on the casing of the Damocles computer in chapters 6 and 13. Artist Alex Okita confirmed it was on purpose: "That was our goal". Source: wiki.oni2.net. canon

  • Wave Motion Cannon: from Marathon to Oni

    Classic Marathon's resources still hold unreleased items: WAVE MOTION CANNON, THE PLANS, ALIEN ENERGY CONVERTER. The weapon was cut, but the "Wave Motion Cannon" later became the biggest gun in Oni and the only one with two fire modes. The name tips its hat to the anime Space Battleship Yamato. Sources: marathon.bungie.org, wiki.oni2.net. datamine

  • "Frog blast the vent core" reaches Oni

    Oni's Comguys have roughly a 4% chance to shout "Okay, okay, don't frog blast the vent core!", a line lifted word for word from Marathon's assimilated BOBs. Source: wiki.oni2.net. canon

  • MIDA Multi-Tool in Destiny

    Destiny's exotic MIDA Multi-Tool rifle is named after MIDA, the rebel movement that controlled Mars in Marathon's lore. Source: attackofthefanboy.com. canon

  • "Fate of All Fools" = Durandal's motto

    Destiny's "Fate of All Fools" and the related Jade Rabbit point to Durandal's Latin phrase "Fatum Iustum Stultorum", the just fate of fools. Source: attackofthefanboy.com. canon

  • Traxus, SERPENT and Titan Arcology

    A popular myth puts the Traxus corporation in Destiny 2. What actually happened: in the 2023 promo ARG for Marathon's reveal, the "Traxus" site hid a "SERPENT" chat room that pointed to the Titan Arcology location in Destiny 2. Traxus itself never appears in Destiny 2. Source: bungie.fandom.com. canon

12.5

Terminals

Lore veterans quoteThirty years of memes

  • Frog blast the vent core!

    The best-known line of panicked gibberish, shouted by a Marathon crew member, a "BOB" (Born on Board). The meme is more than thirty years old. canon One theory has Halo's marines yelling "They're everywhere!" as a habit inherited from the BOBs, who screamed on the run. theory

  • Gheritt White

    A notorious terminal from the first game: a stream-of-consciousness horror piece (a man, knives, water) dropped into the middle of a sci-fi shooter with no explanation. canon Fans still argue about what it was. theory

  • A litany of names

    A terminal monologue runs through other people's names: Roland, Beowulf, Achilles, Gilgamesh, heroes and their doomed loyalty. canon Fans have spent thirty years arguing over whose voice it is: the player's, Durandal's, or something older. It is not Tycho, whatever the internet keeps saying. theory

  • Two sticks and fire

    The Prometheus riddle on the 2026 site's 404 page retells a 1994 terminal: Prometheus is a thief there, and theft drives progress. canon Durandal lives by the same myth. He stole a ship and means to steal godhood. theory

12.6

Cut content

And encodedBoxes, files, logins

  • What the letters on the boxes meant

    The letters on the classic games' boxes were studio in-jokes. DMUKYA (Marathon) stands for "Don't Make Us Kick Your Ass"; MBIBTYB (Marathon 2) for "My Bob Is Bigger Than Your Bob"; TBWSAF on the Trilogy was pieced together from the hate mail Bungie got for releasing on Windows, with the swearing cut by the archive itself. Former Bungie staffer Alex Rosenberg confirmed the readings. canon

  • Aliens that were cut

    Three Pfhor species were cut before release. Hound was a fast melee unit that "acted a lot like piranhas" and guarded the low ground; its textures still sit in the incubator vats on four Pfhor levels. Armageddon Beast, a damage sponge, went because no level made fighting it fun; the unarmed Alien Crewman, a civilian Pfhor, ran into the memory cap on monster types per level. datamine

  • Lovecraft in the walls

    The Cthulhu mythos shows through the trilogy here and there. The level name "Strange Aeons" is a line from Lovecraft himself; on "Son of Grendel" the encoded string ^589222hstrlstherth reads as "Hastur rules the Earth"; and W'rkncacnter (file 08) is cast as a Great Old One, "dead but dreaming" in its burning prison. The new game's Anomaly reads easily in the same key. theory

  • The hidden terminal on "Never Burn Money Again"

    The level files hide a fourth terminal, guarded by an S'pht Compiler, with broken-up text about a woman with a rose and a room that "flickered and tore". Its timestamp reads "core OS mod date later than current date/time", the same time trick Durandal leaves elsewhere, which is why the community credits it to him. theory

  • Jason Jones's login in a terminal

    A terminal on "The Slings & Arrows of Outrageous Fortune" carries the code jon3//12-a, and "jon3" was Jason Jones's real university login in Chicago. The full address is known from Usenet archives and University of Chicago Magazine, but the game never shows the whole domain on screen. Sources: marathon.bungie.org, University of Chicago Magazine. canon

  • Power of Seven: a studio named for the seven

    The Marathon 2 and Infinity themes came from Paul Sebastian's studio, Power of Seven Inc. In May 1995 Headspace Inc. absorbed it and moved it to San Mateo. Source: marathon.bungie.org. canon

  • Durandal lies: thirty versus thirty-one

    On the church level in Venice, Admiral Tfear promises "thirty power switches", yet you have to shut down 31. canon Veterans take this as built-in proof that Durandal fudges the numbers even in his own briefing. theory Source: marathon.bungie.org.

Archive footnote // the number 7

Bungie's sacred number runs through all its games. The Cryo Archive's seven vaults belong with Halo's seven rings, the Power of Seven studio and the 819÷7 gag above. We don't buy the coincidence. theory
