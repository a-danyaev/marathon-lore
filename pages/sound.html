Sound

In Marathon, sound works as a second pair of eyes. Almost everything you do, and everything other players do, can be heard, and heard from FAR away. A new player watches the screen while a veteran listens to half the map and finds them by ear. canon

NEW RUNNERS > rule number one from your very first run: if you can hear them, they can hear you.

06-C.1

Why

Chase Combs // Ryan LottWhy it matters

Bungie built the sound around the world of Tau Ceti IV: the game has no single protagonist, and the soundscape belongs to the place. Audio director Chase Combs and composer Ryan Lott (Son Lux) deliberately keep the music restrained so it doesn't bury the combat sounds you rely on. canon

In an extraction shooter

Footsteps, reloads and exfil timers tell you more than the HUD does; the interface doesn't show everything. Sound warns you before a silhouette appears in view, so listening to the map matters more than precise aim. datamine

06-C.2

Noise

Movement and actionsWhat makes noise

Sprinting is loud, walking is quieter, and crouch-walking is nearly silent. Your gait is the main lever of stealth and the only way to get up close or slip away quietly. Plenty of other actions make noise too: doors, self-healing, weapon swaps, looting, opening loot rooms and calling exfil. datamine

Habit

Before you make noise, work out who you are about to call over.

06-C.3

Knife

A rookie trapSprinting with a blade

Plenty of players sprint with the knife out for the speed, and it costs them. The knife strike itself is quiet, but sprinting with a knife drawn is one of the loudest sounds in the game. It gives your position away from far off, and you run into someone's ambush holding a blade instead of a ready gun. datamine

RULE > to move fast and quietly, put the knife away and don't sprint where someone might be waiting.

06-C.4

Gunfire

A beacon both waysThey hear you

Gunfire carries a very long way, and that cuts both ways. Each of your shots gives away your position, while someone else's fight is free intel: from the firefight you can tell where people are fighting, where to stay away from and who you might pick off weakened. Waiting for the fight to end and taking out the winner is classic third-partying. canon

Suppressor

It shrinks a shot's sound footprint without removing it completely. canon

06-C.5

Listening

Reading the mapSignals, not background

Nearly every sound on the map tells you something. Put them together and you understand the situation before you see anyone. datamine

  • You hearA metallic clang

    A patrol of UESC bots, before you see it.

  • You hearWildlife cries

    Something spooked it: someone is moving nearby, and the cry points toward the threat.

  • You hearUnfamiliar footsteps

    Enemy footsteps sound different from your teammates'.

  • You hearDoors and vaults

    Someone is already working nearby.

06-C.6

Ears

SettingsGear

Headphones beat any speakers: only they give you a sound's direction and distance. Turn the music down and the SFX up. Bungie designed the game to be played by ear and acknowledges the advantage, so there is no "cheating" involved. Knowing where the enemy is often wins you half the fight. canon

  • Musiclower
  • SFXhigher

06-C.7

Darkness

Night Marsh // Season 2Hearing comes first

In Night Marsh (Season 2) visibility is almost entirely taken away, and hearing becomes your MAIN sense. canon

Dread as a mechanic

The game deliberately scares you with sounds you can't identify yet and makes you act blind, by ear alone. The habit of listening to the map on the brighter levels pays off here: players who never built it and relied on their eyes alone die in Night Marsh. datamine

06-C.8

Tuning

A moving targetSubject to change

Sound is a load-bearing mechanic, and Bungie keeps tuning it. One patch pushed gunshot audibility so far that the community nicknamed it a "third-party fest"; the studio admitted it had overdone it and rolled the change back. canon

How to keep it in your head

Don't memorise exact metres and decibels; they will change with the next update. Keep the principle, and check specific values against Bungie's latest official patch notes. After Nightfall Refresh (1.1.9.2, October 6, 2026) the old hearing rules still hold: the patch summaries we checked list no sound changes. theory our check

TL;DR // run checklist

  1. Crouch near objectives and doors.
  2. Don't sprint with the knife out toward a fight or an exfil.
  3. Shoot only when you are ready to be heard.
  4. Listen before every corner.
  5. Headphones on, music down.
  6. If you can hear them, they can hear you.
